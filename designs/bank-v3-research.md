# Bank Heist v3 - Real-World Research Brief

Setting: 1990s St Petersburg, Janus syndicate bank, GoldenEye 007 PC-port mod (generated level).
Purpose: ground v3 of the bank level in real bank architecture and security practice.

## 0. How to read this document

- Every factual claim carries a bracketed source number; the numbered list is at the end.
- Where a number could not be found in a source I opened, I say so ("not found") and, if I offer a number anyway, it is tagged **[DESIGN ESTIMATE]**. Those are my own game-scale suggestions, not research results.
- Research gaps (honest list):
  - I did NOT find HABS/HAER measured floor plans with dimensions for a specific bank. The LoC record for the Owatonna bank (HABS MN-85) lists photographs and data pages only [1]; an MIT record lists a 1908 main-floor plan, upper-floor plan and cross-section of the banking room [2], but no figures.
  - I found no authoritative source for realistic headcounts per role, for guard tour intervals as a standard, or for armoured-car crew protocol. Those sections are labelled accordingly.
  - Round vault door diameters were not found in any source. Weights and thicknesses were.
  - Interior layouts of the St Petersburg bank buildings (operating halls, skylights) were not found in English sources; only addresses, architects and dates.

### 1.2 Owatonna (Sullivan, 1908)

- HABS MN-85: 22 photographs, 8 colour transparencies, 4 data pages; construction 1907-08 with later work c.1930 and 1958 [1].
- Open-plan banking room with stencilling, terra-cotta and plaster ornament and four cast-iron electroliers; Elmslie did much of the interior [1][2].
- Useful to the game: the banking room is one big visible volume; staff and public share the same sightlines, which is exactly the security posture of a daytime bank (see 3.1).

## 2. Vaults

### 2.1 Construction and doors

| Item | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Walls (old vaults) | Steel-reinforced concrete, at least 1 ft (0.3 m) thick; door typically 3.5 ft (1.1 m) thick; whole vault weighing hundreds of tons | [3] |
| Walls (mid-20th c.) | ~18 in (45.7 cm) | [3] |
| Hatton Garden walls | 50 cm (20 in) per Wikipedia; press reports said up to 2 m, sources disagree | [7][8] |
| Nice 1976 vault | Walls over 1 m thick, steel doors 500 mm thick | [14] |
| Nice 1976 alt. claim | 1.8 m concrete wall (RivieraRadio) | [15] |
| Baker St vault floor | 3 ft (0.91 m) reinforced concrete | [12] |
| Antwerp vault | Two floors below the main floor | [11] |
| Kansas City Fed door (Mosler, 1921) | 50-ton door, $32,000; moved on two house-mover trucks pulled by 4 horses plus two 5-ton trucks | [4] |
| Winnipeg Bank of Montreal (1913) | Basement vault doors 50 tonnes, "thickest and heaviest in the world" at construction | [26] |
| Mosler door, 1928 newspaper | Net solid steel thickness 15 in through body, about 21 in on the edge with bolt and glass frames; hung on a cast-steel ball-bearing crane hinge | [5] |
| Winona National Bank (Diebold) | 24 bolts, 22.5 short tons; four-point closing system presses door with about 1/3 of its weight | [3] |
| Round doors | Popular early 20th c.; fell out of favour because of manufacturing complexity, door sag from weight, and cost | [3] |
| Diebold | Sold the then-largest commercial bank vault to Detroit National Bank in 1921 (no size given) | search summary, not independently opened |
| Diameter | **Not found** in any source | - |

Vault ratings: UL Class M 15 min, I 30 min, II 60 min, III 120 min; breach = 96 sq in hole or disabled bolts. EN 1143-1 grades 0-XIII [3].

### 2.2 Day gate, time lock, ventilators

| Item | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Day gate | A second door inside the vault door frame, often open mesh or glass; "limited protection while the main door is open"; meant to keep out casual visitors, not real security | [3] |
| Day gate at Antwerp | Pried open with a crowbar once the vault door was open | [11] |
| Time lock | Clock that prevents opening until a set number of hours has passed even with the correct combination | [3][6] |
| Origin | First proven 29 May 1875 at Great Barrington MA, when robbers holding a banker's family hostage could not get the vault opened | [6] |
| Number of movements | "Usually three time locks per door; first to reach 0 allows access, others are backups" (flagged citation-needed on Wikipedia) | [6] |
| Setting | Set under dual control by two authorised staff at closing (credit-union procedure); no vault opening before the stated hour, e.g. not before 7:00 am | search summary of a credit-union procedure, URL below [40] |
| Time lock vs. police | In the Baker Street job police checked the vault door on 12 Sep and could not open it because it was time-locked, so the break-in went unnoticed until bank staff opened it on 13 Sep | [12] |
| Emergency ventilator | Time-locked vault cannot be opened from inside and a trapped person may suffocate; UL 680 covers "emergency vault ventilators" intended to give fresh air to people locked in by accident or during a robbery | [32][33] |
| Ventilator as weak point | Patent text notes ventilators "may readily enable entry of explosives", so the walls and jambs around them are reinforced | [32] |

### 2.3 Safe-deposit boxes and the dual-key system

| Item | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Two locks, one bolt | Most safe-deposit locks are two locks in one housing controlling one bolt | [19] |
| Guard key | Bank's key, kept by the "vault custodian"; one bank key can work every box lock in a bank | [19] |
| Renter's key | Customer's key; both keys must be turned, guard key first | [19] |
| Why | Gives bank oversight of customer access | [19] |
| Antwerp | 189 boxes; thieves broke open 109 with a hand-cranked custom device | [11] |
| Baker St | 268 boxes opened, about a quarter of the branch's boxes; main safe not attempted | [12] |
| Coupon / viewing booths | The term "coupon room" appears for the Winnipeg basement ("safe deposit coupon and storage rooms") [26]; I found no more detail on booths | [26] |

### 2.4 Cash processing, night depository, bays

- Cash rooms (where notes are sorted and counted) are closed to customers and outsiders [40]; vault and shipments run under dual control [40].
- Counting practice from a forum post: one counts strap totals aloud, the other records, then swap and recount [40] (forum, low quality).
- Night depository: no authoritative source found. Not covered.
- Bullion bay: the Northern Bank had a loading bay where a white van picked up cash on trolleys in two trips [16].

## 3. Security design

### 3.1 Security layers table (real practice, then how to use it)

| Layer | Real practice | Source | Game use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perimeter/site | Soane: blank screen wall, light from above | [31] | Few ground-floor windows; skylight/roof route |
| Entry vestibule / mantrap | Two-door chamber, one door unlocked at a time; first must close and lock before second releases; used by banks, data centres, courthouses | [36] | Double-door entrance that locks the player in on alarm |
| Teller line | Ballistic glazing over the counter, ballistic fibreglass below, deal trays, a "passer", a bullet-resistant door; glazing 8-10 lb/sq ft, big panels 400+ lb | [20] | Teller windows are cover but not walls |
| Barrier heights | "Six or more feet" above the counter is a common design; non-rated "jump shields" add height | [20] | Glass to ceiling optional; add a jump-shield gap |
| UL 752 levels | L1 9mm, L2 .357 Magnum, L3 .44 Magnum, L4 .30-06, L5 7.62 mm, L7 5.56x45, L8 7.62 NATO; banks traditionally chose L1, L3 now more common; L4/5 for exterior windows | [20][21] | Teller glass survives pistols (PP7, KF7) but rifles chew through it |
| Cameras | Interior and exterior | [18] | CCTV room with monitors |
| Vault alarm | Vibration/seismic detectors react to hammers, drills, saws, thermal tools within a 3-14 m radius; early vibration vault alarms patented by ADT 1961-64 | [34][35] | Drilling the wall triggers a timer, not instant fail |
| Antwerp stack | Combination lock, infrared thermal sensor, magnetic-field lock plates, ceiling light sensor, seismic sensor, Doppler radar, magnetic field detector, day gate | [11] | Template for a multi-sensor vault |
| Dual control | No employee can enter or reach currency alone; vault codes split so no one person holds both; each vault entry logged and initialled by two | [18] | Two keycards / two NPCs needed |
| Time delay | Vault time lock | [3][6] | Vault door literally cannot open until the "hour" |

### 3.2 Opening and closing procedure (what is actually documented)

From Regulation H guidance (US) [18] and procedure summaries [40]:

1. Written opening and closing procedures covering facility, vaults, teller drawers, negotiable instruments [18].
2. Dual control: nobody enters the building or reaches currency alone [18].
3. "All-clear signal" and a minimum of two employees on the premises [18]. How the signal works is not described in any source I found. The common practice (first employee scans outside, enters, signals, second waits) is general knowledge, **not sourced**.
4. Vault opened under dual control, not before a set hour; vault timer set under dual control by two authorised staff [40].
5. Vault entries/exits logged with times, initialled by two [18].
6. Train all staff in robbery conduct, with before/during/after steps [18].
7. Audit and test devices periodically [18].
8. Time locks exist because they defeat the "kidnap the keyholder" attack [6]; that is the exact attack used at Northern Bank (see 6).

## 5. Guard practice

| Item | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Watchclock | Guards carry a mechanical clock (about 5 lb) in a leather pouch; at each checkpoint a numbered key, chained to the station, is inserted and turned, stamping its number onto a paper disk or tape beside a pre-printed time | [22] |
| Record length | Disk: about 24 h; tape up to 96 h; supervisor unlocks and replaces at end of period | [22] |
| Tamper sign | Clock perforates the disk each time the case opens; more than two perforations means it was opened / records may be forged | [22] |
| Hourly rounds | A Detex station at the Otis Mill (Maine, 1927 Newman model) recorded the guard's hourly rounds | [23] |
| Station layout | Small metal boxes with hinged lids, mounted near doors, key on a 12-inch chain | [22] |
| History | Detex (formerly Newman) founded 1878; mechanical production ended 31 Dec 2011; electronic systems replaced them (from 1990 in North America) | [23][22] |
| Intervals generally | No universal standard; set by post orders; "some areas hourly, others less"; route time has to fit inside the interval or more guards are needed | [38] |
| Radio check-ins | **Not found**; set by site policy | [38] |
| Shift change | Knightsbridge: the shift change came one hour after the robbers left, which is when the crime was discovered | [17] |

Game notes:
- A 1990s bank in Russia would plausibly have mechanical clocks or early electronic wands; either way, the missed checkpoint is the natural alarm trigger (AI idea: if the roving guard misses a station, a supervisor NPC investigates).
- Alarm response is a recurring failure in the cases: Hatton Garden's alarm fired at 00:21 and police did not respond [7]; Nice's test alarm drew no response [14].

Common causes across the cases:
1. Holiday/weekend windows (Hatton Garden, Antwerp, Nice, Fortaleza, Baker Street) [7][11][12][14][9].
2. Alarms that were off, ignored, or unanswered [7][12][14].
3. Insider or social access (Northern Bank, Knightsbridge, Antwerp) [16][17][11].
4. Entry from below or beside, not through the front door (tunnels, sewers, lift shaft) [9][12][14][7].
5. Communications leaks (Baker Street radio) [12].

## 7. Design principles for v3

1. **The vault sits below the banking hall**, reached through a guarded stair and a day gate: real vaults are below grade or at the rear (Winnipeg basement vaults, Antwerp two floors down) [26][11]; one chokepoint also makes the heist legible.
2. **Banking hall is the hero space**: double height, colonnade, gallery on all sides, a skylight or roof light [26][31]. One big room ringed by service rooms matches the St Petersburg description [28].
3. **Teller line is cover, not a wall**: ballistic glazing and fibreglass below the counter are rated against pistols (L1-L3) not rifles [20][21].
4. **Entrance vestibule doubles as a mantrap**: two doors, one open at a time [36]; use it for a lockdown scripted event.
5. **Vault door needs a "time"**: a time lock stops opening before a set hour even with correct codes [3][6]; make the door a timer objective, not just a keycard.
6. **Day gate sits behind the main door**: a mesh/glass barrier that is cheap to defeat but stops casual walking in [3][11].
7. **Dual control for everything valuable**: two keyholders, split codes, two-person logging [18]; require two keys/cards or two NPCs at vault, cash room and safe-deposit counter.
8. **Safe-deposit wall uses guard key + renter key**: the custodian's key is the mission key, and boxes are optional loot [19].
9. **Seismic / vibration zone around the vault**: drills and thermal tools are detected within metres [34]; drilling is an alternate loud route with a countdown, as Hatton Garden did in 50 cm walls [7].
10. **Layered sensors, each a mini-puzzle**: copy Antwerp's stack of lock, IR, magnetic, light and day gate [11].
11. **At least one alarm is disabled or ignored**: Baker Street's trembler alarms were off, Hatton Garden's alarm went unanswered, Nice's drew no response [12][7][14]; use it as a hidden weakness the player can discover, not a flaw to fix.
12. **Emergency vault ventilator exists and is a weak spot**: UL 680 ventilators and their reinforced ports are real [33][32]; a small alternate vent route (or a locked-in-hostage subplot).
13. **Armoured-car bay with its own gate** separate from public doors: a van entering the bullion bay is both Northern Bank's exit and a typical delivery point [16][37].
14. **Guard patrols have checkpoints**: a roving guard turning keys or wands on an hourly-style route, tamper/missed stations raising suspicion [22][23]; fixed posts at door, hall, vault corridor.
15. **Shift change is a window**: Knightsbridge staff change came an hour after the robbers left [17]; build a hand-over timer into the guard AI.
16. **Dual-control means hostages are the key**: Northern Bank used hostages to make staff cooperate [16]; a "force the manager to open" objective is realistic.
17. **Back-of-house has a sally-port / loading bay, CCTV room, counting room, and stairs**: all closed to the public [40][18].
18. **Under-floor or utility entry gives a second route**: tunnel (Baker Street, Fortaleza), sewer (Nice), lift shaft (Hatton Garden) [12][9][14][7]; a service-tunnel or lift-shaft route is realistic and gives replayability.
19. **Radio chatter is a risk**: the Baker Street gang was undone by an overheard walkie-talkie [12]; an AI that reacts to noise/alarm is faithful.
20. **St Petersburg dressing**: granite palazzo exterior, blank street face, hall lit from above [28][29], stone/marble interior [24][26].
